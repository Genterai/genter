# Literature: adding task-relative context to agent tool responses

Marks: **[V]** = bibliographic record and key claim checked this session (arXiv/ACL/proceedings abstract or publisher page). **[M]** = cited from memory; a detail not checked. "Abstract" means the claim is the authors' own summary, not a re-read of the full text.

## 1. Just-in-time / proactive / zero-query retrieval

- **Rhodes & Maes, "Just-in-time information retrieval agents", IBM Systems Journal 39(3–4):685–704, 2000.** [V] https://www.bradleyrhodes.com/Papers/rhodes-jitir-agents-ibmsj-2000.pdf — Remembrance Agent, Margin Notes, Jimminy surface documents related to the user's current work without a query; users retrieve and *use more* information than with a search engine, not just faster. *Design:* the original evidence that push beats pull for things people would not have searched for; the effect is "more used", not "faster".
- **Dumais, Cutrell, Sarin, Horvitz, "Implicit Queries (IQ) for Contextualized Search", SIGIR 2004 (demo).** [V] https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/SIGIR-SIS20IQ.pdf — queries are generated automatically from the email being read and results shown in a side pane; matching against personal content (Stuff I've Seen, Dumais et al., SIGIR 2003, pp. 72–79 [V]) was important. *Design:* the implicit query is built from the *current object* (the record the agent is reading), not from the user's words — the same shape as Genter deriving a query from a `read`.
- **Shokouhi & Guo, "From Queries to Cards: Re-ranking Proactive Card Recommendations Based on Reactive Search History", SIGIR 2015, pp. 695–704.** [V] https://ir.webis.de/anthology/2015.sigirconf_conference-2015.72/ — first study of zero-query cards (Cortana); engagement varies with time/place; ranking improves most when the user's *reactive* search history is matched to cards. *Design:* the agent's own earlier queries in the session are the best signal for what to push.
- **Samarinas & Zamani, "ProCIS: A Benchmark for Proactive Retrieval in Conversations", SIGIR 2024; arXiv 2405.06460.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06460 — 2.8M Reddit conversations; system must decide *when* to intervene and with what; proposes npDCG, a metric that rewards early and penalises useless interventions. *Design:* a ready-made evaluation idea: score injected context on timing and precision, not only on recall.
- **Deng, Lei, Lam, Chua, "A Survey on Proactive Dialogue Systems", IJCAI 2023 (arXiv 2305.02750)** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02750; extended as **Deng et al., "Proactive Conversational AI: A Comprehensive Survey…", ACM TOIS 2025, DOI 10.1145/3715097** [V]. Proactivity is under-designed in dialogue systems; open problems include when to take initiative and evaluation. *Design:* frames Genter's injection as system-initiated information, a known and unsolved sub-problem.
- **Lu et al., "Proactive Agent: Shifting LLM Agents from Reactive Responses to Active Assistance", ICLR 2025; arXiv 2410.12361.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12361 — ProactiveBench (6,790 events); a reward model trained on human accept/reject labels judges proactive offers; best fine-tuned model reaches F1 66.47%. *Design:* even trained, proactive offers are wrong about a third of the time; acceptance must be measured.
- **Horvitz, "Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces", CHI 1999, pp. 159–166.** [V record; expected-utility rule via secondary sources] https://erichorvitz.com/uiact.htm — act autonomously only when expected value exceeds inaction; named failure modes: poor guessing of goals, poor timing, ignoring the cost of acting. *Design:* every injected item has a cost (tokens, distraction); a threshold, not "always add".

## 2. Active / adaptive retrieval and self-knowledge

- **Jiang et al., "Active Retrieval Augmented Generation" (FLARE), EMNLP 2023; arXiv 2305.06983.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06983 — retrieves when the upcoming sentence contains low-confidence tokens, using that sentence as the query; better or competitive on four long-form tasks. *Design:* retrieval triggered by the model's uncertainty — which an external MCP server cannot see.
- **Asai et al., "Self-RAG", ICLR 2024 (oral).** [V] https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/25f7be9694d7b32d5cc670927b8091e1-Abstract-Conference.html — model trained with reflection tokens decides when to retrieve and critiques passages; beats ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama2-chat on QA, reasoning, fact verification. *Design:* needs training the agent; not available to a tool layer serving arbitrary clients.
- **Su et al., "DRAGIN", ACL 2024 (2024.acl-long.702); arXiv 2403.10081.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10081 — decides when (token uncertainty, influence, semantics) and what (self-attention over the whole context) to retrieve; needs attention scores, so not usable via closed APIs. *Design:* confirms the trigger signals live inside the model; the layer must use *observable* proxies (the query, the records read, the action about to be taken).
- **Jeong et al., "Adaptive-RAG", NAACL 2024 (2024.naacl-long.389); arXiv 2403.14403.** [V] https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.389 — a small classifier routes queries to no / single-step / multi-step retrieval by predicted complexity; better accuracy and efficiency. *Design:* an external, cheap classifier deciding "is extra context worth it here" is a validated pattern.
- **Yin et al., "Do Large Language Models Know What They Don't Know?", Findings of ACL 2023; arXiv 2305.18153.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18153 — SelfAware (1,032 unanswerable / 2,337 answerable questions), 20 LLMs: some self-knowledge, a clear gap to humans. [M: GPT-4 F1 ≈75.5 vs human ≈85] *Design:* agents under-recognise their own gaps, the core premise of "unknown unknowns".

## 3. Distraction by irrelevant or noisy context

- **Shi et al., "Large Language Models Can Be Easily Distracted by Irrelevant Context", ICML 2023; arXiv 2302.00093.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00093 — GSM-IC adds one irrelevant sentence to grade-school maths problems; performance "dramatically decreased"; self-consistency and an instruction to ignore irrelevant information mitigate. *Design:* one off-topic sentence is enough to hurt; label injected context as optional and separate it from the answer.
- **Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts", TACL 12:157–173, 2024.** [V] https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/ — U-shaped accuracy: best when relevant information is at the start or end. [M: GPT-3.5-Turbo with the answer mid-context fell below its closed-book score of 56.1% on NQ.] *Design:* position matters; put injected context in a short, fixed block, not interleaved with results.
- **Cuconasu et al., "The Power of Noise: Redefining Retrieval for RAG Systems", SIGIR 2024; arXiv 2401.14887.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14887 — high-scoring passages that do *not* contain the answer hurt most; random documents can raise accuracy up to 35%. *Design:* the danger is related-but-wrong context (the same customer's other ticket), exactly what similarity-based injection produces.
- **Yoran, Wolfson, Ram, Berant, "Making Retrieval-Augmented Language Models Robust to Irrelevant Context", ICLR 2024; arXiv 2310.01558.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01558 — irrelevant passages cause compounding errors in multi-hop QA; NLI filtering prevents the drop but discards useful passages; ~1,000 fine-tuning examples give robustness. *Design:* a pre-filter (entailment or relevance to the task) is cheap but lossy; measure both harms.
- **Hong, Troynikov, Huber, "Context Rot: How Increasing Input Tokens Impacts LLM Performance", Chroma technical report, July 2025.** [V via secondary summaries only] https://research.trychroma.com/context-rot — 18 models; performance falls with input length; a single semantically similar distractor lowers accuracy. *Design:* industry, not peer-reviewed; supports a hard token budget for injected context.

## 4. Knowledge conflicts and stale beliefs

- **Longpre et al., "Entity-Based Knowledge Conflicts in Question Answering", EMNLP 2021; arXiv 2109.05052.** [V] https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.565 — swapping the answer entity in the passage shows models over-rely on memorised answers; a mitigation improves out-of-distribution generalisation 4–7%. *Design:* a changed fact may be ignored if the model "knows" the old one.
- **Chen, Zhang, Choi, "Rich Knowledge Sources Bring Complex Knowledge Conflicts", EMNLP 2022, pp. 2292–2307.** [V] https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.146 — retrieval quality decides whether models use context or prior; conflicts between passages barely lower confidence. *Design:* when sources disagree, say so explicitly; models will not notice.
- **Xie et al., "Adaptive Chameleon or Stubborn Sloth", ICLR 2024; arXiv 2305.13300.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300 — LLMs accept coherent, convincing counter-evidence, but show confirmation bias when evidence also contains information consistent with their prior. *Design:* a "this changed" notice works when it is clear, dated and sourced; burying it among consistent records invites the old belief.
- **Wu, Wu, Zou, "ClashEval", NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks; arXiv 2404.10198.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198 — six models (incl. GPT-4o) adopt incorrect retrieved content over their correct prior >60% of the time; less so as the change becomes implausible; more when prior confidence is low. *Design:* agents largely believe what tools return, which makes injected context powerful and wrong injections dangerous.

## 5. False presuppositions in the task

- **Yu, Min, Zettlemoyer, Hajishirzi, "CREPE: Open-Domain Question Answering with False Presuppositions", ACL 2023; arXiv 2211.17257.** [V] https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.583 — 25% of real Reddit questions carry false presuppositions; models detect presuppositions but struggle to verify them, largely for lack of retrieved evidence. *Design:* the gap is evidence, not reasoning; the layer supplying the contradicting record is the missing piece.
- **Kim, Htut, Bowman, Petty, "(QA)²: Question Answering with Questionable Assumptions", ACL 2023, pp. 8466–8487.** [V] https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.472 — natural search queries with false/unverifiable assumptions; models handle them poorly. *Design:* a task like "send the renewal to Acme's contact X" may rest on a stale assumption; the layer can surface the record that contradicts it without telling the agent what to do.

## 6. Agents' information-seeking deficits

- **Yao, Shinn, Razavi, Narasimhan, "τ-bench", ICLR 2025; arXiv 2406.12045.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045 — GPT-4o function calling pass^1 61.2% retail, 35.2% airline; pass^8 <25% retail. Of 36 agent failures: ~55% wrong argument/information, ~25% wrong decision (missed domain rules), 19% partial resolution. *Design:* "missed a rule that was in the policy" is a measured failure class, the target of policy injection.
- **Drouin et al., "WorkArena", ICML 2024, PMLR 235:11642–11662.** [V] https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/drouin24a.html — 33 ServiceNow tasks; agents promising but far from automation; large open/closed gap. *Design:* enterprise-system benchmark and BrowserGym harness to reuse.
- **Huang et al. (Salesforce AI Research), "CRMArena-Pro", 2025.** [V figures via Salesforce blog/secondary; first author (K.-H. Huang) M] https://www.salesforce.com/blog/crmarena-pro/ — ~58% single-turn → ~35% multi-turn success; near-zero inherent confidentiality awareness. *Design:* CRM baseline; confidentiality matters for any injected cross-source context.
- **Wang et al., "Learning to Ask: When LLM Agents Meet Unclear Instruction", EMNLP 2025; arXiv 2409.00557.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00557 — agents fabricate missing arguments instead of asking; Ask-when-Needed helps. *Design:* agents fill gaps with guesses.
- **Vijayvargiya et al., "Ambig-SWE", ICLR 2026; arXiv 2502.13069.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13069 — models struggle to tell under- from well-specified tasks; with interaction they improve up to 74%. *Design:* agents do not notice what they are missing; information has to reach them.
- **Laban et al., "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation", arXiv 2505.06120 (ICLR 2026).** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120 — 39% average drop multi-turn vs single-turn; models make early assumptions and prematurely attempt final answers. *Design:* direct evidence of premature closure (satisficing); context must arrive before the agent commits.
- **Krishnamurthy et al., "Can Large Language Models Explore In-Context?", NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2403.15371.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15371 — LLMs fail to explore in bandits unless given an external summary of history. *Design:* under-exploration is general; external summarisation fixes it, an argument for the layer doing the cross-source look.
- **Wei et al., "BrowseComp", arXiv 2504.12516, 2025.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12516 — GPT-4o with browsing ~1.9% (secondary), Deep Research ~51.5%. *Design:* persistent search is a trained skill; generic agents stop early.
- **Simon, "A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice", QJE 69(1), 1955.** [M] — origin of satisficing. *Design:* theory term for "stop at the first plausible answer".

## 7. Context engineering, memory, retrieval for agents

- **Anthropic, "Effective context engineering for AI agents", engineering blog, 29 Sep 2025.** [V] https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents — "smallest possible set of high-signal tokens"; context rot; just-in-time retrieval via references vs pre-retrieval; hybrid recommended. No quantitative evaluation. *Design:* the vendor guidance favours small, high-signal additions; not evidence.
- **Anthropic, "Introducing Contextual Retrieval", Sep 2024.** [V] https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval — prepending chunk-specific context cuts top-20 retrieval failure 5.7%→3.7% (embeddings), 2.9% (+BM25, −49%), −67% with reranking. *Design:* context added at *index* time improves retrieval; different from injection at response time.
- **Packer et al., "MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems", arXiv 2310.08560, 2023.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560 — virtual context management across memory tiers; document analysis and multi-session chat. *Design:* memory paging is agent-side; Genter is the outer tier.
- **Gan & Sun, "RAG-MCP: Mitigating Prompt Bloat in LLM Tool Selection", arXiv 2505.03275, 2025.** [V] https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03275 — retrieving only relevant MCP tool descriptions cuts prompt tokens >50% and raises tool-selection accuracy 13.62%→43.13%. *Design:* in MCP, less context selected for the task beats everything at once.
- **Greshake et al., "Not what you've signed up for", arXiv 2302.12173 (AISec 2023).** [V arXiv; venue via secondary] https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12173 — indirect prompt injection through retrieved data. *Design:* injected Slack/email text is an attack surface; deliver it as quoted data, never as instructions.
- **Gap:** I found **no peer-reviewed evaluation of proactive context injection into MCP/tool responses** for enterprise agents. ProactiveBench and ProCIS are the closest (user-facing, not agent-facing). The comparison planned here would be novel.

## 8. Task-relative relevance

- **Saracevic, "Relevance: A review… Part II: nature and manifestations of relevance", JASIST 58(13):1915–1933, 2007** [V] https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jamist/v58y2007i13p1915-1933.html; Part III (behaviour and effects) 2126–2144 [V]. Relevance is a set of manifestations (system, topical, cognitive, situational, affective). *Design:* "relevant to the query" and "relevant to the task" are different relevances.
- **Borlund, "The concept of relevance in IR", JASIST 54(10):913–925, 2003.** [V] https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jamist/v54y2003i10p913-925.html — situational relevance: usefulness relative to the *work task* underlying the need; objects can be situationally relevant without being algorithmically relevant. *Design:* the policy that blocks the action is situationally relevant but rarely matches the query; injection needs a task model.
- **Wilson, "Situational relevance", Information Storage and Retrieval 9(8):457–471, 1973.** [M; journal later renamed Information Processing & Management] — relevance relative to a person's situation, stock of knowledge and goals. *Design:* the concept of "relevant to what you're doing, whether or not you'd recognise it".
- **Belkin, "Anomalous states of knowledge as a basis for information retrieval", Canadian Journal of Information Science 5:133–143, 1980.** [V record] — people cannot specify what they do not know; IR should model the anomaly, not the query. *Design:* the theoretical root of "unknown unknowns": the agent's query cannot express the need injection serves.

## Implications

1. **The need is real and measured.** Agents miss rules (τ-bench ~25% wrong-decision failures), commit early (Laban: −39%), under-explore (Krishnamurthy) and do not notice under-specification (Ambig-SWE, SelfAware). They will not ask for what they don't know is missing.
2. **Agents believe tool output** (ClashEval >60% override). Injection will change behaviour; precision is a safety property, not a nicety.
3. **The danger is related-but-wrong context**, not random text (Cuconasu; Chroma; Shi). Similarity-based "related items" are the worst case; filter by task, entity and freshness.
4. **Budget and position.** A small, fixed, clearly labelled block at the end of the response (Liu; Anthropic; RAG-MCP), with a hard cap and a rule to inject nothing when nothing passes the threshold (Horvitz).
5. **Use observable triggers.** FLARE/DRAGIN use internal uncertainty the layer cannot see; use what it can see: the query, the anchors read, the write about to happen. An Adaptive-RAG-style classifier for "worth adding?" is validated.
6. **Changes must be explicit.** A stale fact contradicted quietly may lose to the model's prior (Xie, Longpre); state "X changed from A to B on date, source" and note disagreements between sources (Chen).
7. **Presupposition checks, not advice.** CREPE shows the missing piece is the contradicting evidence; the layer can show the record that contradicts the task's assumption and stop there.
8. **Situational ≠ topical relevance** (Borlund, Saracevic, Belkin). Constraining policies rarely match the query; injection needs the task (action + entity), not only the query string.
9. **Security and confidentiality.** Injected cross-source text is a prompt-injection vector (Greshake) and can leak information (CRMArena-Pro). Mark it as quoted data with source and permissions.
10. **Evaluate like proactive IR.** Score timing and precision (ProCIS npDCG, ProactiveBench acceptance), harm on tasks where injection is irrelevant (GSM-IC style controls), and reliability (τ-bench pass^k) across the three arms: source tools, cross-source search, injected context.
11. **Novelty.** No rigorous public evaluation of agent-facing context injection in MCP/enterprise settings was found; the planned comparison would fill that gap.
