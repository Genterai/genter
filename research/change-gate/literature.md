# Literature review: mathematically gating an LLM agent on change in an embedding stream

Context: every update (Slack, email, CRM, docs, tickets) is embedded with `text-embedding-3-small` (1536-d, unit-norm; OpenAI's `dimensions` parameter allows Matryoshka-style truncation, per OpenAI's Jan 2024 "New embedding models and API updates" post). Goal: decide *mathematically* when a change is significant enough to start an agent.

Verification legend: entries marked (v) were checked against arXiv / ACL Anthology / PMLR / AAAI / NeurIPS / official docs during this review. Entries marked (m) are well-known classics cited from memory (venue and year are reliable; I did not re-open the paper).

## 1. Semantic novelty / novelty detection on text embeddings

- **Sun, Ming, Zhu, Li. "Out-of-Distribution Detection with Deep Nearest Neighbors." ICML 2022. arXiv:2204.06507** (v) <https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06507>. Score = distance to the k-th nearest neighbour in the training set. No distribution is assumed. On ImageNet it cuts FPR95 by 24.77% compared with SSD+. L2-normalizing features matters a lot: the ablation reports a 61.05% FPR95 improvement.
  *Gate:* the kNN distance to a reference memory of past embeddings is the simplest per-item novelty score. OpenAI vectors are already unit-norm, so the normalization step is free.
- **Lee, Lee, Lee, Shin. "A Simple Unified Framework for Detecting Out-of-Distribution Samples and Adversarial Attacks." NeurIPS 2018. arXiv:1807.03888** (m). Uses class-conditional Gaussians on features and scores with Mahalanobis distance.
  **Podolskiy et al. "Revisiting Mahalanobis Distance for Transformer-Based Out-of-Domain Detection." AAAI 2021. arXiv:2101.03778** (v) <https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03778>. Mahalanobis distance on Transformer utterance embeddings beats the other OOD methods on intent datasets (+1–5% AUROC).
  *Gate:* a per-source (or per-cluster) mean and covariance with Mahalanobis distance accounts for anisotropy, which raw cosine does not. 1536-d needs shrinkage or PCA to get a stable covariance.
- **Breunig, Kriegel, Ng, Sander. "LOF: Identifying Density-Based Local Outliers." SIGMOD 2000** (m). **Liu, Ting, Zhou. "Isolation Forest." ICDM 2008** (m). LOF scores density relative to neighbours. iForest scores how easily random partitions isolate a point.
  *Gate:* LOF handles mixed-density data, for example a dense Slack chatter cluster next to sparse contract docs. iForest is cheap but weak in very high dimensions without reduction.
- **Allan, Carbonell, Doddington, Yamron, Yang. "Topic Detection and Tracking Pilot Study: Final Report." DARPA Broadcast News Workshop 1998** (m). **Allan, Lavrenko, Jin. "First Story Detection in TDT Is Hard." CIKM 2000** (m). TDT defined first-story detection (FSD): flag a document as new when its maximum similarity to every earlier document is below a threshold. The 2000 paper argues FSD is inherently hard, with poor precision for this approach.
  *Gate:* this is the original "novel if far from everything seen" rule, and its known weakness (many false alarms) is the reason a single-item threshold should not trigger the agent alone.
- **Petrović, Osborne, Lavrenko. "Streaming First Story Detection with application to Twitter." NAACL-HLT 2010, N10-1021** (v) <https://aclanthology.org/N10-1021/>. Uses LSH-based nearest-neighbour search for constant-time FSD. It runs more than 10x faster with comparable accuracy and was applied to 160M tweets.
  *Gate:* this is a proven streaming architecture (approximate NN plus a novelty threshold plus thread grouping) that maps directly onto a vector index.
- **Ghosal, Saikh, Biswas, Ekbal, Bhattacharyya. "Novelty Detection: A Perspective from Natural Language Processing." Computational Linguistics 48(1), 2022** (v) <https://aclanthology.org/2022.cl-1.3>. Treats document-level non-novelty as multi-premise entailment, not as similarity.
  *Gate:* real novelty ("adds information") is an entailment question. Embedding distance is only a proxy.
- **Bates, Candès, Lei, Romano, Sesia. "Testing for Outliers with Conformal p-values." Annals of Statistics 2023. arXiv:2104.08279** (m). Turns any novelty score into a valid p-value and controls FDR across many items with Benjamini–Hochberg.
  *Gate:* calibrated per-item p-values make it possible to bound the false-alarm rate at "X per day".

## 2. Data / distribution drift in embeddings

- **Gretton, Borgwardt, Rasch, Schölkopf, Smola. "A Kernel Two-Sample Test." JMLR 13, 2012** (m). The MMD statistic with permutation or asymptotic null distributions.
  *Gate:* this is the standard window-vs-reference test for "has the distribution of embeddings changed".
- **Rabanser, Günnemann, Lipton. "Failing Loudly: An Empirical Study of Methods for Detecting Dataset Shift." NeurIPS 2019. arXiv:1810.11953** (v) <https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11953>. Across many shifts, the best approach was two-sample testing on representations reduced by a pretrained model (BBSD with univariate KS plus Bonferroni, and MMD on reduced dimensions). Domain classifiers helped most to *characterize* a shift.
  *Gate:* reduce dimensions first (PCA, or truncated Matryoshka dimensions), then test.
- **Ramdas, Reddi, Póczos, Singh, Wasserman. "On the Decreasing Power of Kernel and Distance Based Nonparametric Hypothesis Tests in High Dimensions." AAAI 2015. arXiv:1406.2083** (v) <https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2083>. The power of MMD and energy-distance tests falls polynomially with dimension against fair alternatives. The paper also analyzes the median bandwidth heuristic.
  *Gate:* run MMD on the full 1536 dimensions only with care. Reduce first.
- **Lopez-Paz, Oquab. "Revisiting Classifier Two-Sample Tests." ICLR 2017. arXiv:1610.06545** (m). Train a classifier to tell window from reference. Held-out accuracy gives the test, and the classifier is interpretable.
  *Gate:* the classifier's most confident window items explain *what* changed, which is useful input for the agent.
- **LSDD: Bu, Alippi, Zhao. "A pdf-Free Change Detection Test Based on Density Difference Estimation." IEEE TNNLS 2018** (m; Alibi Detect cites "Bu et al. (2017)") is built on Sugiyama et al.'s least-squares density difference (Neural Computation 2013, m).
- **Cobb, Van Looveren, Klaise. "Sequential Multivariate Change Detection with Calibrated and Memoryless False Detection Rates." AISTATS 2022, PMLR 151:226–239. arXiv:2108.00883** (v) <https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/cobb22a.html>. Online MMD and LSDD with time-varying thresholds set by simulation to hit a target **expected run time (ERT)** with a constant false-positive rate. Fixed thresholds are shown to be badly miscalibrated.
  *Gate:* this is the closest off-the-shelf match to an "alarm budget". "One false alarm per N updates" becomes a parameter.
- **Alibi Detect** (v) <https://docs.seldon.ai/alibi-detect>. Offline detectors: KS, CVM, MMD, LSDD, classifier and learned-kernel. Online detectors: `MMDDriftOnline`, `LSDDDriftOnline` and `CVMDriftOnline`, all configured by `ert` and `window_size`. Univariate CVM uses a Bonferroni-like correction that assumes independent features.
- **Evidently** (v) <https://docs-old.evidentlyai.com/user-guide/customization/embeddings-drift-parameters>. The default embedding drift method is "model" (domain classifier, ROC AUC > 0.55). The alternatives are "ratio" (share of components drifted under per-component Wasserstein, default 0.2), "distance" (Euclidean or cosine distance between mean embeddings) and MMD.
- **NannyML** (v) <https://nannyml.readthedocs.io/en/main/how_it_works/multivariate_drift.html>. PCA **reconstruction error** fitted on reference data raises an alert past 3σ of the reference. Also offers a domain-classifier AUROC.
  *Gate:* PCA reconstruction error catches directions the reference never spanned, a cheap signal for "new kind of content".
- **Sequential detectors.** **Page. "Continuous Inspection Schemes." Biometrika 1954** (m): CUSUM and Page–Hinkley. **Bifet, Gavaldà. "Learning from Time-Changing Data with Adaptive Windowing." SDM 2007** (m): ADWIN, with rigorous false-positive and false-negative bounds on an adaptive window over a univariate stream. **Kifer, Ben-David, Gehrke. "Detecting Change in Data Streams." VLDB 2004** (m).
  *Gate:* these work on a *univariate* summary such as mean novelty score, outlier rate or cluster-share, not on raw vectors.
- **Li, Xie, Dai, Song. "M-Statistic for Kernel Change-Point Detection." NeurIPS 2015. arXiv:1507.01279** (later titled "Scan B-Statistic…") (v) <https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.01279>. Online kernel change-point detection built from block MMD (B-test) against plentiful background data. An analytic tail approximation sets thresholds without bootstrap.
  *Gate:* a streaming MMD with an analytic ARL-to-threshold mapping, cheaper than permutation tests.
- **Embedding-specific text drift.** Feldhans et al. "Drift Detection in Text Data with Document Embeddings." IDEAL 2021, LNCS, doi:10.1007/978-3-030-91608-4_11 (v, bibliographic only; I did not read the content). See also arXiv:2309.10000, "Detecting covariate drift in text data using document embeddings and dimensionality reduction" (v, title only).

## 3. Concept drift vs data drift

- **Gama, Žliobaitė, Bifet, Pechenizkiy, Bouchachia. "A Survey on Concept Drift Adaptation." ACM Computing Surveys 46(4), 2014** (m). Defines real concept drift as a change in p(y|X), virtual drift as a change in p(X), and catalogs patterns: sudden, gradual, incremental, recurring.
- **Lu, Liu, Dong, Gu, Gama, Zhang. "Learning under Concept Drift: A Review." IEEE TKDE 31(12), 2019 (online 2018). arXiv:2004.05785** (m). Organizes the field into drift detection, understanding (when, how much, where) and adaptation.
  *Gate:* with unlabeled embeddings, only **virtual drift / covariate shift p(X)** is observable. "Significance for the business" is a latent y. Embedding drift can show *that* content changed, but not that a *fact* changed. A deal moving from "closed-won" to "lost" can barely move the embedding. That case needs a structured-field check, or an LLM or NLI judge, on the candidates the gate passes.

## 4. Changes in the distribution of local anomalies / conformal change detection

- **Vovk, Nouretdinov, Gammerman. "Testing Exchangeability On-Line." ICML 2003** (m). Conformal p-values for each item feed a betting martingale. Under exchangeability, P(sup M ≥ C) ≤ 1/C (Ville's inequality).
- **Volkhonskiy, Burnaev, Nouretdinov, Gammerman, Vovk. "Inductive Conformal Martingales for Change-Point Detection." COPA 2017, PMLR 60:132–153. arXiv:1706.03415** (v) <https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03415>. ICM-based detectors need only the i.i.d. assumption before the change, with no change model, unlike CUSUM or Shiryaev–Roberts. They are competitive with those methods.
- **Vovk, Petej, Nouretdinov, Ahlberg, Carlsson, Gammerman. "Retrain or not retrain: Conformal test martingales for change-point detection." COPA 2021, PMLR 152. arXiv:2102.10439** (v) <https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10439>. The decision is literally "retrain or not", with guaranteed validity, on top of any predictor.
  *Gate:* the most principled design available. Use a kNN or LOF nonconformity score against a calibration set, compute a conformal p-value per update, and fire when a martingale (or CUSUM on −log p) crosses 1/α. This turns a "rate of local outliers is rising" signal into a statistic with a provable false-alarm bound. Caveat: workplace streams are not i.i.d. (weekly cycles, bursts). Condition on source or time of week, or recalibrate on a rolling basis.

## 5. Emergence of new clusters in streams

- **Zhang, Ramakrishnan, Livny. "BIRCH." SIGMOD 1996** (m). **Aggarwal, Han, Wang, Yu. "A Framework for Clustering Evolving Data Streams" (CluStream). VLDB 2003** (m). **Cao, Ester, Qian, Zhou. "Density-Based Clustering over an Evolving Data Stream with Noise" (DenStream). SDM 2006** (m). **Hahsler, Bolaños. "Clustering Data Streams Based on Shared Density between Micro-Clusters" (DBSTREAM). IEEE TKDE 2016** (m). All four keep micro-clusters (cluster-feature summaries) with time decay. DenStream distinguishes potential from outlier micro-clusters, and an outlier micro-cluster that gains weight is promoted. That promotion is a natural "new topic emerged" event. Implementations exist in `river` (Python) and MOA.
  *Gate:* "a promoted micro-cluster" is a better trigger than any single far-away item. It requires several mutually close novel items, which suppresses one-off noise.
- **Kleinberg. "Bursty and Hierarchical Structure in Streams." KDD 2002** (m). An automaton (two-state HMM) over arrival rates detects bursts.
  *Gate:* use it for the arrival-rate side, for example a cluster suddenly receiving 5x its usual rate.
- **Blei, Lafferty. "Dynamic Topic Models." ICML 2006** (m). Hoffman, Blei, Bach. "Online Learning for LDA." NeurIPS 2010 (m).
- **Grootendorst. "BERTopic: Neural topic modeling with a class-based TF-IDF procedure." arXiv:2203.05794, 2022** (m). Docs (v): `partial_fit` for online learning with incremental clusterers, and `merge_models` (v0.16, Nov 2023) to merge a small model trained on new data into a base model. Duplicate topics merge and new topics are added. The two cannot be mixed after `.fit`, and merged models must share an embedding model.
  *Gate:* the merge path (cluster the new window, compare its topic embeddings with existing ones, report the topics that did not match) is a ready "new-topic" detector. It also gives human-readable c-TF-IDF labels to hand to the agent.

## 6. Limitations of embeddings and embedding-based retrieval

- **Weller, Boratko, Naim, Lee. "On the Theoretical Limitations of Embedding-Based Retrieval." arXiv:2508.21038 (2025), ICLR 2026** (v) <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21038>. The number of distinct top-k subsets a single-vector model can return is bounded by the embedding dimension, and the bound holds even with free-parameter embeddings optimized on the test set. On the simple LIMIT dataset, SoTA models score below 20 recall@100.
  *Gate:* geometric nearness cannot represent every combination of relevance. A gate built only on vector geometry will miss some changes by construction.
- **Weller, Lawrie, Van Durme. "NevIR: Negation in Neural Information Retrieval." EACL 2024. arXiv:2305.07614** (v) <https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.139>. When two documents differ only by a negation, many SoTA retrievers rank no better than random. Cross-encoders do best and bi-encoders worst.
  *Gate:* "we will renew" versus "we will not renew" can sit at cosine ≈ 0.95+. Embedding novelty will *not* fire on reversals.
- **García-Ferrero, Altuna, Álvez, Gonzalez-Dios, Rigau. "This is not a Dataset: A Large Negation Benchmark to Challenge Large Language Models." EMNLP 2023. arXiv:2310.15941** (v) <https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.531>. About 400k true/false sentences, two-thirds with negation. LLMs handle affirmatives well and negatives poorly, and fine-tuning does not generalize well.
  *Gate:* even the LLM judge downstream of the gate is fallible on negation.
- **Xu, Lin, Sun, Chang, Indyk. "SparseCL: Sparse Contrastive Learning for Contradiction Retrieval." arXiv:2406.10746; ICML 2025** ("Contradiction Retrieval via Contrastive Learning with Sparsity") (v) <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10746>. Cosine similarity is ill-suited to contradiction because it is transitive-ish (paraphrases look close). The paper combines cosine with Hoyer sparsity and gains 11.0% on average. Cross-encoders are at least 200x slower.
  *Gate:* contradiction against existing knowledge needs NLI or a cross-encoder on the top-k neighbours, not a distance threshold.
- **Steck, Ekanadham, Kallus. "Is Cosine-Similarity of Embeddings Really About Similarity?" arXiv:2403.05440, 2024** (v; WWW'24 companion per third parties, not confirmed). In regularized linear models, cosine similarity can be arbitrary or non-unique.
- **Anisotropy.** **Ethayarajh. "How Contextual are Contextualized Word Representations?" EMNLP 2019. arXiv:1909.00512** (m). Contextual representations occupy a narrow cone, so random pairs have high cosine. Remedies: **Li et al. "On the Sentence Embeddings from Pre-trained Language Models" (BERT-flow), EMNLP 2020** (m) and **Su et al. "Whitening Sentence Representations…" arXiv:2103.15316, 2021** (m).
  *Gate:* use no absolute cosine threshold. Calibrate against the empirical distribution of each corpus and source (percentiles or conformal scores), or whiten first. I did not find a peer-reviewed measurement of anisotropy for `text-embedding-3-small` specifically. Measure it on our data.
- **Radovanović, Nanopoulos, Ivanović. "Hubs in Space." JMLR 2010** (m). **Beyer et al. "When Is 'Nearest Neighbor' Meaningful?" ICDT 1999** (m). In high dimensions, distances concentrate and some points (hubs) are near everything.
  *Gate:* kNN novelty scores need rank- or percentile-based calibration, and hubness-aware scaling may help.
- **Numbers and dates.** **Wallace, Wang, Li, Singh, Gardner. "Do NLP Models Know Numbers? Probing Numeracy in Embeddings." EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019, D19-1534. arXiv:1909.07940** (v). Magnitude is encoded only roughly, the linear decodability holds for small ranges (< 100) and degrades beyond, and subword models like BERT are less exact.
  *Gate:* "$50k → $500k" or "due 3 May → 3 June" moves the vector very little. Diff the extracted numbers and dates structurally.
- **Temporal retrieval.** **Chen, Wang, Wang. "A Dataset for Answering Time-Sensitive Questions" (TimeQA). NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks. arXiv:2108.06314** (m). **"MRAG: A Modular Retrieval Framework for Time-Sensitive Question Answering" (introduces TempRAGEval). arXiv:2412.15540** (v, abstract-level): every retriever tested performed poorly under temporal perturbations. **TEMPO benchmark, arXiv:2601.09523 (2026)** (v, abstract-level; preprint): the best of 12 retrievers reached 32.0 NDCG@10.
  *Gate:* embeddings do not encode "newer supersedes older". Recency and supersession must be explicit metadata (timestamps, versions), not geometry.

## 7. Industrial practice: event-driven / ambient agents

- **Harrison Chase (LangChain). "Introducing ambient agents." Blog, 14 Jan 2025** (v) <https://blog.langchain.com/introducing-ambient-agents>. Agents that listen to event streams and act on them, with human-in-the-loop patterns notify / question / review. The reference implementation is an email assistant on LangGraph.
- **Sean Falconer (Confluent). "The Future of AI Agents Is Event-Driven." BigDATAwire, 26 Feb 2025; republished on the Confluent blog** (v) <https://www.confluent.io/blog/the-future-of-ai-agents-is-event-driven/>. Argues agent scaling is a data-infrastructure problem, and proposes Kafka/Flink event streams with orchestrator-worker patterns.
- **Change-data-capture: Debezium** (m) <https://debezium.io/>. Log-based CDC that emits row-level change events to Kafka. This is the standard substrate for "trigger on data change".
  *Gate:* industry triggers agents on *events*, usually rule- or filter-based. I found **no published industrial system that gates an LLM agent on a statistical test over embedding drift**. Our design would be novel, so it should be benchmarked against simple baselines (rules, counts, per-item LLM triage).

## Implications for the gate

1. **Detect at three levels and combine them.** (a) Per-item novelty: kNN distance or Mahalanobis to a reference memory, calibrated to conformal p-values. (b) Emergence: a new micro-cluster (DenStream/DBSTREAM) or an unmatched topic after a BERTopic merge. (c) Distribution shift: a windowed MMD/LSDD or classifier two-sample test. Fire on (b), or on (c), or on a run of (a). A single far-away item should rarely fire on its own, because of the FSD false-alarm history.
2. **Calibrate. Never use a raw cosine threshold.** Anisotropy and hubness make absolute values meaningless. Use per-source percentiles or conformal p-values, optionally after whitening.
3. **Budget false alarms explicitly.** Online MMD/LSDD with a target ERT (Cobb et al. 2022, Alibi Detect) or a conformal test martingale (Ville bound 1/α) turns "agent runs at most ~N times per week on noise" into a parameter.
4. **Reduce dimensions before testing.** Use PCA or truncated Matryoshka dimensions (for example 128–256). The power of kernel tests falls with dimension (Ramdas et al. 2015), and Failing Loudly found that dimensionality reduction followed by testing works best.
5. **Monitor univariate summaries with classic sequential tests.** Run ADWIN, Page–Hinkley or CUSUM on outlier rate, mean novelty, per-cluster arrival rate (Kleinberg bursts) and PCA reconstruction error (NannyML). They are cheap and well understood.
6. **Partition by source and condition on seasonality.** Slack and contracts have very different densities, and workplace streams have weekly cycles that break exchangeability. Keep per-source references, or a time-of-week calibration set, and refresh them on a rolling basis.
7. **The embedding gate is blind to some of the most important changes.** Negation, reversals, contradictions, number and date edits, and supersession barely move the vector (NevIR, SparseCL, Wallace et al., TempRAGEval, LIMIT). Add a cheap second channel: a structured diff of extracted entities, numbers, dates and status fields, plus NLI or a cross-encoder between the new item and its top-k neighbours.
8. **Treat drift as virtual (p(X)) evidence only.** Without labels, "significant to the business" is unobservable (Gama 2014, Lu 2018). The gate should pass *candidates*. Collect agent and human verdicts on fired events as labels, then tune thresholds (and possibly learn a triage classifier).
9. **Hand the agent explanations, not just an alarm.** Classifier two-sample tests, cluster c-TF-IDF labels and the nearest prior neighbours of novel items say *what* changed and reduce agent cost.
10. **Evaluate against baselines.** No industrial precedent for statistical embedding gating was found. Benchmark precision and recall of fired events against rule triggers and against cheap per-item LLM triage on a labeled replay of real streams (DriftBench-style).
